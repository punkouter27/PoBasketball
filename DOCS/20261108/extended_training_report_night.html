What happened overnight, in plain words

Scramble (getting a loose ball back) went from 0 % to 79 % on its real test. The pass bar is 80 %, so it missed by one percentage point (158 of 200 episodes; it needed 160). Before tonight the agent stood still and never got near the ball. With the new reward for moving the palm toward the ball, it first learned to approach (about 90 M steps in), then to touch, and then to recover the ball. Falls dropped from 30 % after stage 2 to 7 % after stage 4. Stage 4 recorded zero bad (NaN) gradient batches. Stage 2 had a burst of 20 at 187 M steps, and stage 4 continued from that run. The weights were not exported to production: the rules need the pass bar plus 100 successful episodes in a row.

Dribble did not pass. The new push reward taught the agent to actually bounce the ball, not just hold it: successful bounce cycles in training rose from about 1 % to a peak of 34 %, and episodes doubled in length. It then plateaued at a return of about 300, and the bounce rate drifted back down to about 14 %. On the real test, both dribble stages scored 0 %. The agent stays upright (2.5 % falls) but loses the ball after about one bounce, the same as before tonight.

Recommended next steps

  1. Scramble: one more short run (about 30 min) from l3b_scramble_n9q2, with gradient clipping. That should push it past 80 % and the 100-in-a-row check. Then export it and run the Unreal 5 s rollout check.
  2. Dribble: stop adding PPO hours on this reward. Train against the scripted hand–ball reference motion (imitation), using ckpt_75M.pt from l2a_push_n9q1 (the checkpoint nearest the 34 % bounce peak) as the start.
